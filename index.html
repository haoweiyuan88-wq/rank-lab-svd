What changes when you move k?

A photo as a matrix

Each grayscale pixel has a brightness from 0 to 255. An image with m rows and n columns stores m × n values. In color mode, red, green and blue each have their own matrix.

The original is displayed in high resolution. To keep the slider responsive, the reconstruction, pixel inspector and storage counts use an SVD grid of up to 384 pixels on its longer side.

Rebuilding an image one term at a time

Singular value decomposition writes the image as A = UΣVᵀ. The rank-k image is the sum of the first k terms, ordered from largest to smallest singular value. The starting image A0 is all zeros. At row r and column c, term i contributes σi × ui[r] × vi[c].

Ak = k∑i = 1 σi ui viT

Reading the live equation

Move from 5 to 6 to add term 6. Move back to subtract it. A jump from 5 to 20 adds terms 6 through 20. Tap the original to see the contribution at one row and column. For a color image, choose a channel in the equation.

Pixel calculations show rounded approximations to the underlying values. The displayed image clips values to the range 0–255 and rounds them to whole numbers.

When does SVD save storage?

Each term stores two vectors, of lengths m and n, and one singular value. Keeping k terms needs k(m + n + 1) values per channel. This saves storage when that count is smaller than mn.

Try 5, then 20. Which detail can you recognize now? The change map shows where the added terms brightened or darkened the image.
